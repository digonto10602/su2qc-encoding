### Is `dropped_total` genuine or an artefact of our summation?

It is the laptop engine's own number, passed through unchanged: `rishon.verify` takes `Batch.dropped_total` of one batch of 1296 columns and adds no sum of its own (`sparse_sim.py` lines 36-45: $\sqrt{\sum_c d_c^2}$, each $d_c$ the sum over coalesce events of the square root of the dropped weight). Measured (script `tester-216-probe_drop.py`, 64 trial columns, then $\times\sqrt{1296/64}$; branching gates counted after the engine's lowering, `tester-216-probe_counts.py`):

| circuit | branching (H/sx/u) gates after lowering | native `ccx` | `dropped_total` full run (1296 col.) | per-column rms $d_c$ (64 col.) | $d_c$ per branching gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| general (ccx macros) | 600 | 1402 | 3.68e-13 | 1.03e-14 | 1.7e-17 |
| general-rccx | 3840 | 54 | 2.91e-12 | 7.9e-14 | 2.1e-17 |
| general-mcx | 3516 | 216 | 2.69e-12 | 7.3e-14 | 2.1e-17 |
| qiskit_l3 | 3458 | 0 | 3.79e-12 | 1.39e-13 | 4.0e-17 |

Conclusion (measured): the bound grows linearly with the number of **Hadamard-type branching gates in the lowered circuit** (each leaves a $\sim10^{-16}$ rounding residue that is dropped and tallied by the triangle inequality); the three seeds that lower to H have the same per-gate rate to 20 %, the windowed Qiskit circuit about 2x higher (guess: its Hadamards sit in wider superpositions). So it is a genuine property of the circuit's H count under the laptop's rule, not a summing artefact of ours. It is a conservative bound: it adds residues linearly (triangle inequality) and then multiplies by $\sqrt{1296}=36$ columns in the Frobenius sum; the true simulation error of the same circuits is the block error, $3.5\times10^{-13}$, about 10x smaller. A native `ccx` is a permutation in the engine and drops nothing: a circuit of 4700 `ccx` (random wiring, compute/uncompute pairs, `tester-216-probe_native.py`) has `dropped_total` exactly 0.0, and 700 `rccx` pairs (1400 H) already give $1.19\times10^{-12}>10^{-12}$.

## 7. Probe (e): `prune_budget(spec)`

`prune_budget(rishon_spec("step")) = (4700, 710)` = floor-to-two-digits of $10^{-12}/2.1\times10^{-16}=4761.9$ and $10^{-12}/1.4\times10^{-15}=714.3$ (PRUNE_FRACTION $10^{-2}\times$ `tol_err` $10^{-10}$ = $10^{-12}$). METHODS-rishon R11 says "$\le700$ `rccx` (or $\le4500$ `ccx`)": the same quantities rounded down further, so the two are **consistent** (R11 is the more conservative statement; $4500<4700$, $700<710$). Notes on what the numbers mean (not a failure):
1. The rates `PRUNE_RATES` are the **U_B** rates (my recomputation: $2.154\times10^{-16}$ per `ccx`, $1.408\times10^{-15}$ per `rccx` for UB at $\beta=0.61$, matching the constants). On the **step** (the kind the spec is rendered for) the same circuits give $2.62\times10^{-16}$ and $1.80\times10^{-15}$ (x1.22 and x1.28), so the cap allows about 3800 `ccx` or 560 `rccx` in the general-circuit mix, not 4700 / 710 (`tester-216-prune-rates-ub-vs-step.json`).
2. "Per `ccx`" is a regression proxy: a native `ccx` has zero drop (section 3), and 700 `rccx` pairs alone exceed the cap ($1.19\times10^{-12}$, i.e. about $1.7\times10^{-15}$ per `rccx`). The physical quantity is the number of lowered Hadamard-type gates, about $1.2$-$1.6\times10^{3}$ at the cap.
