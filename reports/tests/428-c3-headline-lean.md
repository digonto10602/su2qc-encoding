### The T-first tie-break for fuzzy_ub (`r13-F3-013` against `r11-F2-011`)

The rule is `_pick` in `src/su2plaq/crosscheck/report.py` (not in `compress/robust.py`; the final-report table uses it): the T-first row is the minimum of
$$\big(K_2,\,K_1,\,K_3,\,K_4,\,K_5,\ \text{laptop key},\ \text{id}\big),\qquad K=(\mathrm{CX},\ \textstyle\sum T,\ \rho,\ \sum \mathrm{depth},\ \text{qubits}),$$
i.e. the robust key with its first two components exchanged (sum of $T$ first, then CX), and the remaining components, then the archive (laptop) key, then the entry id as final tie-breakers. It is a deterministic total order (ids are unique; three repeated calls returned the same entry). The headline is the same minimum with the key in its normal order.

The values (read from the recomputed cache):

| entry | $\sum T$ | CX | $\rho$ | $\sum$ depth | qubits | $\bar T$ | laptop key |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `r13-F3-013` | 10400 | 89 | 1 | **25084** | 10 | 520.0 | (89, 514, 1242, 10) |
| `r11-F2-011` | 10400 | 89 | 1 | 25164 | 10 | 520.0 | (89, 514, 1246, 10) |

Both have the same $\sum T$ (hence $\bar T=520.0$), CX and $\rho$; the fourth component (sum of $\mathrm{depth}_{CT}$ over the 20 pairs) decides: 25084 < 25164. So `r13-F3-013` wins on depth, not on the id and not on the laptop key. The same rule on the headline side: the headline `r10-F1-010` has two exact ties in the robust key with `r10-F3-010` and `r11-F3-011` (all (83, 12688, 0, 30902, 10), same laptop key (83, 638, 1546, 10)); the id breaks that tie (`r10-F1-010` < `r10-F3-010` < `r11-F3-011`). Both T-first entries have $\rho=1$ (class `clifford_offset`).
