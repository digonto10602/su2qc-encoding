Jargon, once: *compression loop* = the Gu-et-al procedure in which an LLM agent ("proposer") rewrites a circuit-building program and a deterministic evaluator scores it; *MAP-Elites* = an archive that keeps the best solution per cell of a coarse cost grid, so that several qualitatively different designs survive; *seed* = a known circuit put into the archive before any proposer runs; *key* = the lexicographic cost $(\mathrm{CX},T_{10^{-6}},\mathrm{depth}_{\rm CT},\mathrm{qubits})$; *skeleton* = the list of gate names and qubits of a circuit with the angles removed; *shim* = a small module of ours that makes the laptop's code accept our encoding at run time without editing the laptop's files; *monkeypatch* = replacing a function inside an imported module at run time; *Walsh series* = expansion of a function of bits in the characters $(-1)^{s\cdot y}$; *phase walk* = $R_z$ rotations on one qubit between CNOT toggles, which implements a Walsh series; *rccx* = relative-phase Toffoli (3 CX, 4 T); *pins* = SHA-256 fingerprints of the exact states and targets compiled into the Lean checker so that a certificate cannot bring its own target.

## 3. The task the proposers face (numbers from Report 3 and the prototype)

Operator: $U=U_B(\beta)U_E(\alpha)$ on the 16-qubit spin-network register (8 data: 4 link bits $j_l$, 4 corner bits $J_c$; 8 outer bits read-only). On the 47 admissible inner codes $U_B=e^{-i\beta K}$ with $K$ = 8 bright $2\times2$ pairs (couplings $-2$ for $0000\leftrightarrow1111$, $-\tfrac12$ for $0101\leftrightarrow1010$, $+1$ for the six other complements; all 16 codes with corner bits $0$) and 31 dark codes (any corner bit set; $K$ row $=0$). $U_E=\prod_l p(-\tfrac34\alpha)$ on the link bits (0 CX). The outer register never needs to be read (one block, M3-B2); reads are allowed by the spec.

Round-0 keys at $(0.37,0.61)$, $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$, full mode through the laptop's `evaluate_circuit` (measured in the prototype, all `valid`, skeleton equal at the three angle pairs):

| seed | CX | $T_{10^{-6}}$ | depth$_{\rm CT}$ | qubits (incl. 8 outer) | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| general (`build_step`, `GENERAL7`) | 54 | 566 | 861 | 23 | M3, `results/spinnet/step.json` |
| general + polish | 54 | 534 (estimate; polish removed 0 gates) | 67 (lowered) | 23 | laptop `passes.polish` |
| Qiskit L3 (windows of 32, seed 12345) | 54 | 534 (estimate) | 67 | 23 | laptop pattern; no gain on this circuit |
| `spinnet_v2` `pairs9` + our $U_E$ | **34** | 832 | 1597 | 19 | su2qc-trotter, vendored in M3 |
| `spinnet_v2` `flags4` + our $U_E$ | 44 | 554 | 869 | 19 | idem |
| `spinnet_v2` `flags5` + our $U_E$ | 42 | 606 | 1009 | 19 | idem (measured in a second prototype run, 15.5 s under the tester's load) |
| `spinnet_v2` `flags6` + our $U_E$ | 36 | 658 | 1120 | 19 | idem (10.3 s) |
| Paulo 15-qubit file ($U_B$ only, $\beta=0.05$, fixed angle) | 68 | 316 ($U_B$ alone) | 90 (lowered) | 23 | `refs/paulo/…gates.txt`, valid with the recorded sign gauge |

Consequence of the cost order (CNOT first): the round-0 best key is `pairs9`'s $(34, 832, 1597, 19)$, not the general circuit's. A proposer wins only with $<34$ CX, or $34$ CX and $<832$ T. The T column is dominated by rotations ($\approx63$ T each at $10^{-6}$, Ross–Selinger): 4 electric + 9 walk rotations for `pairs9` versus 4 + 4 for the general circuit. At equal CX, fewer rotations win.

Where the CX go in the known designs (so the families have something to attack): frame $2\times3$ CX; bright/class flags via `rccx` (3 CX each; 14 in `GENERAL7`, 6 in `pairs9`); walk toggles (2 CX per Walsh term beyond the first). `pairs9` avoids the bright-flag ancilla by folding corner NORs and paying 9 rotations.

## 4. Established methods the families can use (sources read)

1. **Gu, Kemeny, Yelin, Lukin, "Discovering state-of-the-art quantum circuits with large language models", arXiv:2610.22367** (abstract page fetched 2026-11-06: evolutionary framework, LLM proposes, independent evaluator scores; 7 of 8 tasks improved Toffoli and qubit counts). The loop structure (spec hand-text, round briefs with parent + alternatives, DISCOVERY notes, MAP-Elites archive) is the laptop's implementation of its Methods / Extended Data Fig. 1; nothing changes for `spinnet` except the spec instance.
2. **MAP-Elites** — Mouret & Clune, "Illuminating search spaces by mapping elites", arXiv:1504.04909 (archive = one elite per cell of a user-chosen feature grid; here cells $(\lfloor8\log_2(\mathrm{CX}+1)\rfloor,\lfloor8\log_2(T_\varepsilon+1)\rfloor)$, laptop's `archive.py`). AlphaEvolve (Novikov et al., arXiv:2506.13131, §2) uses the same MAP-Elites + island program database with LLM mutation — the precedent for "one proposer per family, migration every third round".
3. **Walsh-series phase walks** — Welch, Greenbaum, Mostame, Aspuru-Guzik, "Efficient quantum circuits for diagonal unitaries without ancillas", New J. Phys. 16, 033040 (2014), arXiv:1306.3991, Sec. 2–3: any diagonal unitary is $\exp(i\sum_S\theta_S\bigotimes_{j\in S}Z_j)$; with a Gray-code ordering of the Walsh terms each term costs one $R_z$ and at most 2 CNOT. For us the phase function is $\theta(y)=2\beta k(y)\,[y\ \text{bright}]$ on the 8 frame bits; its minimal Walsh support (with the dark/invalid slots free) sets the rotation count of an ancilla-free walk, and each rotation costs $\approx63$ T at $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$. The twelve loop's discoveries (`compress/discoveries/twelve.md`: MILP over free points, Gray cycles, in-walk `rccx` toggles with a residual CZ fix) are the same arithmetic on a harder function.
4. **Relative-phase Toffolis** — Maslov, "Advantages of using relative-phase Toffoli gates with an application to multiple control Toffoli optimization", Phys. Rev. A 93, 022311 (2016), arXiv:1508.03273: `rccx` (3 CX, 4 T) is exact in a compute/uncompute sandwich around a diagonal (Lean `Su2Plaq.sandwich`, METHODS §11.1 g); the laptop's `Builder.mcx` chains cost $6+6(k-2)$ CX.
5. **T count per rotation** — Ross & Selinger, "Optimal ancilla-free Clifford+T approximation of z-rotations", QIC 16, 901 (2016), arXiv:1403.2975: $T=3\log_2(1/\varepsilon)+O(\log\log(1/\varepsilon))$ in the typical case; measured with pygridsynth 1.2.0 (METHODS §7): 62–64 at $10^{-6}$. This is why the key's T column moves in steps of $\approx63$.
6. **Phase folding / T-par** — Amy, Maslov, Mosca, "Polynomial-time T-depth optimization of Clifford+T circuits via matroid partitioning", IEEE TCAD 33, 1476 (2014), arXiv:1303.2042: phase-polynomial view of CNOT+$R_z$ networks; the twelve loop used it (r05-F4-005) to show $T_{\rm fixed}$ was fold-optimal for its skeleton. Proposers may use the same reasoning to prove a family's T floor.
7. **Qiskit level-3 transpile** (Qiskit 2.5.2 docs, `transpile(optimization_level=3)`): used only as a windowed baseline (laptop's `qiskit_l3`), because the whole-circuit pass hoists Hadamards and becomes unverifiable on the sparse engine (audit 003).

Not found / not used: no published CNOT lower bound for a multiplexed 4-qubit Pauli rotation with a NOR-gated angle; the plan does not state a numeric target for the loop. Ideas beyond these sources would be the proposers' own and must be labelled as such in their DISCOVERY lines.

## 5. Lean for M4

- **Per-circuit certificates.** The laptop's checker is generic except for the pins (§1). Plan: `lean/Su2PlaqCert/SpinNet/{Pins,Check,Main}.lean` (ours) reuse `Su2PlaqCert.{K,Laurent,State,Gates,Circuit,Check(.checkRange/.blockOf/.gramErrorOf),Json,Sha256}` and replace only the pin constants: SHA-256 of the 433 spin-network unit states (data|outer integer $y+256o$, $K$-coefficient 1) and of the exact route-B targets `UE`, `UB`, `step` orders 1 and 2 at base $(d_\alpha,d_\beta)=(4,4)$ (order 2: $(8,4)$). The target is exactly rational: within each outer block, identity on dark codes and $\begin{pmatrix}\cos\beta k&-i\sin\beta k\\-i\sin\beta k&\cos\beta k\end{pmatrix}$ on a bright pair with $k\in\{-2,-\tfrac12,+1\}$, i.e. $\cos\beta k=\tfrac12(z^{4k}+z^{-4k})$, $-i\sin\beta k=-\tfrac12(z^{4k}-z^{-4k})$ with $z=e^{i\beta/4}$ (integer exponents $\pm2,\pm4,\pm8$); $U_E$ contributes $z_\alpha^{-3n}$, $z_\alpha=e^{i\alpha/4}$, $n$ = links at $\tfrac12$. All seeds are angle-linear with denominators $\le8$ (measured `angle_fractions`: `pairs9` $\in\{\pm\tfrac14,\pm\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac34\}\beta$, `flags4` $\{-\tfrac52,\tfrac32,2,-1\}\beta$, general $\{-\tfrac72,-1,3,\tfrac32\}\beta$, electric $-\tfrac34\alpha$), so `certified` is expected for every seed; a proposer's circuit with emission-time irrational angles gets `n_a` (prompts/006 §8 decision 2: allowed, reported).
- **Structure library** already kernel-checked in M3/M3x (`lean/Su2Plaq/SpinNet/{Counts,Circuit,Recoupling,Semantics,Spectrum,Bridge}.lean`): 47/16/31/8 counts, 433 states, couplings $2K\in\{-4,-1,+2,0\}$ from the 6j symbols, spectrum multiplicities $16/96/16/177/16/96/16$, exact semantics of `build_UB`. M4 renders them into the spec as "Proved structure (Lean)" with `proved_by = decide` and the theorem names, so proposers can rely on exactly these facts.
- **Lemma files** next to programs: the laptop's `lemmas.check_program_lemmas` accepts imports of `Su2Plaq.*` (hence our `Su2Plaq.SpinNet.*`), 120 s each, informative.

## 6. Prototype results (`scratch/planner/proto_m4_shim.py`, measured)

- Registration alone: `default_spec("spinnet","step")` → $(0.37,0.61)$, cap 48 qubits; `oracle.target` equals `SpinNet.target` exactly (0.0); `Builder(spec)` registers `d=[0..7]`, `o=[8..15]`.
- `evaluate_circuit(build_step(0.37,0.61,1,with_outer=True))`: trial 2.7 s (error $1.1\times10^{-14}$), full 3.8 s, key $(54,566,861,23)$.
- `program_source` of the general circuit: 65-line angle-linear program; rebuilt in-process it has the same skeleton. **Subprocess worker: fails** with `unknown encoding 'spinnet'` (the one blocker).
- `spinnet_v2` `pairs9`/`flags4` behind our $U_E$: valid, skeleton equal, keys $(34,832,1597,19)$ / $(44,554,869,19)$; `program_source` works for both (62 / 87 lines).
- `polish`: 0 gates removed; windowed L3: valid, no change; `verify_synthesised` of the general step: ok, 8 rotations, 566 T, error $2.38\times10^{-6}\le8.0\times10^{-6}$, 0.4 s.
- Whole prototype 14.6 s on 10 cores at load; peak RSS well under 2 GB.
