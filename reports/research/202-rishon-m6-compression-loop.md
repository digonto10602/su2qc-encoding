Jargon, once. *Compression loop* = the Gu–Kemeny–Yelin–Lukin protocol (arXiv:2610.22367): an LLM *proposer* writes programs that build circuits, a deterministic *evaluator* decides validity and cost, an *archive* keeps the best valid circuits per cost cell (MAP-Elites), and *rounds* iterate. *Inner view* = verifying a rishon circuit on the 1296 valid inner codes instead of the 38 432 physical states (METHODS-rishon R6). *Prune cap* = the rule that a verdict is rejected (`pruning_bound`) when the sparse simulator's own certified error bound `dropped_total` exceeds 1 % of the error tolerance (prompts/005). *rccx* = Qiskit's relative-phase Toffoli (Margolus gate): a Toffoli up to phases on some basis states, 3 CNOT + 4 T instead of 6 CNOT + 7 T. *Laurent certificate* = the laptop's exact per-circuit Lean check: every amplitude is a polynomial in $z=e^{i\theta}$ and $z^{-1}$ over the number field $K=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$, so one check proves the circuit for all angles (prompts/006 §1 M4).

## 2. Round-0 seeds for rishon (what exists, what is justified)

There is no Paulo or su2qc-trotter circuit on rishon codes (`RishonEncoding.import_map` returns a reason for all four sources). Round 0 therefore contains:
1. **general** — `build_step(α, β, 1, with_outer=True)` with the Toffoli (`ccx`) control ladders (Report 5: 26 touched qubits, 10 102 CX, $T_{\rm fixed}$ 10 218, 166 rotations, $T(10^{-6})$ 20 650).
2. **general + polish** — the laptop's two exact rewrite rules (`cancel_inverse_pairs`, `merge_adjacent_rotations`, both Lean-certified) on it; the general circuit already cancels adjacent self-inverse gates, so the polish may change nothing (recorded either way).
3. **Qiskit L3** — windowed level-3 transpile (windows of 32 instructions, seed 12345), the laptop's definition; whole-circuit L3 as a counts-only row.
4. **general-rccx** — the same circuit with the `ccx` ladders replaced by `rccx` ladders (Maslov arXiv:1508.03273 Fig. 3, gates 2–10: a Toffoli up to a relative phase, CNOT-count optimal; exact here because every ladder is a compute/uncompute pair around a diagonal-on-the-ancilla operation, and the relative phases cancel — the laptop's kernel-checked `rccx_crx_rccx_eq_ccrx` is this fact for one AND). Measured exact (block error $3.4\times10^{-13}$) but rejected by the prune cap (§3): it is evaluated and **recorded in round 0 as what it is** (valid or `pruning_bound`), never special-cased.
5. **general-mcx** — the `Builder.mcx` ladder style ((k−2) rccx + ccx + (k−2) rccx), the construction proposers get when they call `Builder.mcx`; same status as 4.

Nothing else is justified: a 3-qubit "codeword" register would be a different encoding, and the order-2 step is a different task kind.

Reading. `dropped_total` grows linearly with the number of AND gates: about $2.1\times10^{-16}$ per `ccx` and $1.4\times10^{-15}$ per `rccx` (Frobenius bound over all 1296 input columns, prompts/005 §1), because the `rccx` lowering ($H$, $T$, CNOT) leaves rounding residues of order $10^{-16}$ in branches the engine then drops and tallies. The true error (block error) is 3–7× below the cap in every row. So: (i) the cap, not physics, rejects the two cheaper *uncompressed* variants (−32 % CX); (ii) a **compressed** circuit is far from the cap: the laptop's best twelve circuit used 14 `rccx` (reports/tests/041), and at the measured rate the cap allows about 700 `rccx` or 4 500 `ccx` — any circuit worth archiving is below that. Decision in the plan: the rule of prompts/005 stays in force unchanged (it lives in laptop code and was Digonto's decision); the spec tells proposers the measured rates so that they can budget; the cap is **recorded as the reason** whenever it decides a verdict, and a decision item asks Digonto whether a rishon-specific cap (10 % of `tol_err`, needing a one-line laptop keyword) is wanted. Nothing in our code loosens or bypasses the cap.

## 4. Lean inside the rishon loop: what is possible

**Why the laptop per-circuit certificate is `n_a` for rishon.** Two independent reasons, both read from the source: `Check.lean` requires exactly 433 states (`physicalDim`), and the Laurent ring has two formal variables while the rishon $U_B$ needs three: $z_\alpha=e^{i\alpha/d_\alpha}$, $z_\beta=e^{i\beta/d_\beta}$ and $z_\gamma=e^{i\sqrt2\beta/d_\gamma}$ (the bright pair with $\kappa=4\sqrt2$ is the closed block $\{\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(|{\rm vac}_A\rangle+|{\rm vac}_B\rangle),|\ell\rangle\}$, R4; the rotation angle $8\sqrt2\beta$ is intrinsic to the model). Since $\sqrt2$ is irrational, $\beta\mapsto(e^{i\beta},e^{i\sqrt2\beta})$ is dense in the torus, so $z_\beta$ and $z_\gamma$ are algebraically independent over $K$: an identity of three-variable Laurent polynomials implies the identity of functions for every real $(\alpha,\beta)$ — the same argument the laptop uses for two variables (`Laurent.lean` header). The amplitudes of the pair vectors are in $\{\pm1,\pm\tfrac1{\sqrt2},\pm\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac14\}\subset K$ (R4), so the inner target $V_{\rm in}=U_B^{\rm in}U_E^{\rm in}$ has entries in $K[z_\alpha^{\pm1},z_\beta^{\pm1},z_\gamma^{\pm1}]$:
$$U_B^{\rm in}=\mathbb 1+\sum_{p=1}^{81}\Big[(\cos\kappa_p\beta-1)(u_pu_p^{\mathsf T}+v_pv_p^{\mathsf T})+i\sin\kappa_p\beta\,(u_pv_p^{\mathsf T}+v_pu_p^{\mathsf T})\Big],\qquad U_E^{\rm in}=\mathrm{diag}\,z_\alpha^{-3\#Q},$$
with $\cos\kappa\beta=\tfrac12(z_\beta^{\kappa d_\beta}+z_\beta^{-\kappa d_\beta})$ for $\kappa\in\{1,2\}$ and $\tfrac12(z_\gamma^{4d_\gamma}+z_\gamma^{-4d_\gamma})$ for $\kappa=4\sqrt2$ (the 81 blocks have orthogonal supports, so the sum form is exact and sparse: 990 dark diagonal entries plus 81 two-by-two blocks). The states of the inner view are the 1296 computational codes (one term each), far smaller than the 433 twelve-qubit states with up to 17 terms.

**Cost estimate.** The laptop certified the 1500-gate twelve general step on 433 superposition states in seconds to minutes (prompts/006 §7). The rishon general $U_B$ has 4 549 macro gates (≈ 12 000 after lowering) on 1296 single-code inputs; each input stays sparse (a frame opens at most a few branching qubits and is undone before the next pair; the float engine does it in 6.4 s, reports/benchmarks/200). Exact $K$-arithmetic is 10–100× slower than float64 → minutes, within the 900 s limit of the slow test; compressed circuits are 5–50× smaller.

**Design (plan §2.8, step 6.L2).** A track-owned, Mathlib-free, compiled checker `Su2PlaqCert.Rishon.{Laurent3,Gates3,Check3,Main}` (namespace `Su2PlaqCert.Rishon`, reusing the laptop's `Su2PlaqCert.K`), its own executable `su2plaq_rishon_cert` (one `[[lean_exe]]` line appended to `lean/lakefile.toml`, listed for the merge notes), certificate schema `su2plaq-rishon-cert3-1` with `base = {d_alpha, d_beta, d_gamma}` and exponent triples. Trust level: *native-certified* (the executable is compiled Lean; the theorem files in `lean/certs/` use `native_decide`, which the Lean reference describes as "uses the native code compiler (`#eval`) to evaluate the `Decidable` instance, admitting the result via an axiom" that "depends on the correctness of the Lean compiler"). Structure proofs that already exist and are listed in the spec for the proposers: counts (kernel), JW string-freeness (kernel), $H_B$ bright-pair structure (native), exact $U_E$ semantics (kernel), corner Gauss kernels (kernel) — reports/tests/206, 209–212.

## 5. What the twelve loop found that transfers (compress/discoveries/twelve.md), and what does not

Transferable principles (all are things a proposer may rediscover; the spec does not prescribe them):
- Frames: map each two-level block to a pair of codes differing in one bit, rotate on that bit, undo. The rishon general circuit already does this per pair with a greedy control set; the twelve winners shared one frame for all blocks and multiplexed the angle over a few flag bits (Walsh/parity-network form: Welch–Greenbaum–Mostame–Aspuru-Guzik arXiv:1306.3991 Eq. (7): a diagonal unitary is a product of exponentials of Walsh (Pauli-$Z$ string) operators, each one CNOT ladder + one $R_z$; Gray-code walks share the ladders).
- Where the T cost sits: every AND (Toffoli) costs 7 T, `rccx` 4 T (Maslov 1508.03273); the twelve floor $T_{\rm fixed}=104$ was AND-dominated. Gidney's temporary-AND (arXiv:1709.06648: 4 T to compute, 0 T to uncompute) does not apply here because its uncompute measures the ancilla and the project forbids measurement; the `rccx` compute/uncompute pair is the measurement-free analogue the evaluator accepts.
- Phase folding / T-count floors (Amy–Maslov–Mosca arXiv:1303.2042, phase-polynomial view): the twelve loop used it to argue optimality of its skeleton; useful for the proposers' negative results.

Not transferable: outer reads (twelve's flags used outer $q_j$ bits; the rishon inner codes carry the link types themselves, and the plan disables outer reads, R6 item 2), the GHZ fold of the four $q_j$ bits (rishon has no single "spin bit" per link; the type is a 4-bit code), and the 433-state dark/bright classification (rishon: 990 dark codes, 81 pairs on 1296 codes, R4).
