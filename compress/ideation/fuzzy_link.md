# Ideation: `fuzzy_link` / `UE` (4 qubits, no ancilla)

Structure used by both families. With A = X2X4 - Y2Y4 + Z2Z4 (= 4|Phi+><Phi+|_{24} - 1),
H = alpha/2 K1 + kappa2 K2 = const - alpha/8 A (1 + Z1Z3) - kappa2 X1X3 + kappa2 Y1Y3 A.
That gives exactly 10 Pauli strings, all diagonal in the Bell(1,3) x Bell(2,4) basis. In frame bits
(a1,b1,a2,b2), where cx(q1,q3) h(q1) and cx(q2,q4) h(q2) put XX_13 -> Z_a1 and ZZ_13 -> Z_b1 (same for 24),
the parities are
{a2, b2, a2b2, b1a2, b1b2, b1a2b2, a1, a1b1a2, a1b1b2, a1b1a2b2}. Three have weight 1 and seven do not.
Coefficients: alpha/8 on the 6 K1 strings and kappa2 on the 4 K2 strings, so the circuit is
Lean-certifiable (rational multiples of alpha and kappa2). The oracle column 0 is supported on {0,5,10,15},
which agrees with this pair-Bell structure.

Exhaustive checks (scratch/proposer/fl_bfs.py, fl_frames.py, fl_net.py):
- I computed the shortest CX-only parity network I -> I that covers the 10 parities in the symmetric Bell
  frame by BFS over GL(4,2) x coverage. It needs **9 CX**.
- I tried every CX frame L1 with at most 3 CX followed by a per-qubit Clifford that diagonalizes all
  10 strings (18 distinct parity sets). Total = 2*cx(L1) + net. The best is **13** (two symmetric 2-CX Bell
  frames). Every 3-CX frame gives 14-16.

## 1. `bell_parity_network` (most promising): expected CX 13
Datapath: cx(q1,q3) cx(q2,q4) h(q1) h(q2), then the BFS-optimal 9-CX parity network with 10 rz (angles
2*alpha/8*sign and 2*kappa2*sign; compute the signs at emission time from the oracle or from the
Pauli expansion), then the inverse frame. Total 13 CX and 10 rotations (about 600 T at eps=1e-6).
It beats the 30-CX seed and the roughly 14-CX bound because the frame is split per pair, and because the
network visits the 7 non-trivial parities in an order where only 2 CX are spent returning to identity.
To implement, store the BFS path (record parent pointers in fl_bfs.py) and place each rz when its row
first equals a target parity.

## 2. `frame_network_fusion`: expected CX 11-12 (refinement of 1)
Datapath: same Bell frame plus a phase polynomial, but the network may end at a linear map E != I when
E is generated by CX gates inside the H-wrapped pair {q1,q2} or inside {q3,q4}. A CX between two
H-wrapped qubits is a reversed CX outside, so E can be pushed through the closing H layer and merged with
the closing frame CX layer (cx(L1^-1 E') may cost <= 2). The first and last network CX can also cancel
against frame CX when they act on (q1,q3) or (q2,q4) before the H layer is applied. Search: BFS from I
to every E in that subgroup. Score = net(I->E) + cx(L1) + cx(E' L1^-1), with the rotation count fixed
at 10. Lower bound in this model: 2 + 2 frame CX (each Bell pair needs at least one CX on each side to
separate X and Z support) + 7 (one CX per new non-weight-1 parity) = 11.
