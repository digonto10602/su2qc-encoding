Terms (first use). **Compression loop**: the Gu-et-al.-style search in which language-model "proposer" agents write circuit-building programs and a deterministic evaluator decides validity and cost (prompts/001 §4). **Unit**: a piece of the step compressed on its own (here two, see §1). **Seed**: the starting circuit of the loop. **Polish**: the evaluator's deterministic clean-up passes (gate cancellation), applied before any proposer runs. **Family**: one circuit architecture, followed across rounds. **Key**: the cost tuple compared lexicographically — CNOT count first, then T count at $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$, then CT-depth, then qubits. **Valid**: exact (op-norm error after one global phase $\le10^{-10}$) on the whole unit block at the nominal angles and at the two alternate angle pairs with an identical gate skeleton (so no angle-specific tricks), all work qubits returned to $|0\rangle$, peak qubits within the cap, evaluation under 300 s. **Lean column**: whether a Lean per-circuit certificate exists; `n_a` = not available (the laptop certificate exporter is specific to the 12-qubit encoding), `not_run` = not attempted for that row.

Terms added 2026-11-19. **Angle pair**: a choice $(\alpha,\beta)$ of the two Trotter angles (for the link unit the pair is $(\alpha,\kappa_2)$, from the same list); the evaluator uses a fixed list of 20 pairs (`ANGLE_SET`) besides the nominal pair $(0.37,0.61)$. **Robust key** of a circuit program: $(\mathrm{CX},\ \sum_{i=1}^{20}T_i,\ \rho,\ \sum_{i=1}^{20}\mathrm{depth}_i,\ \text{qubits})$, compared lexicographically, where $T_i$ and $\mathrm{depth}_i$ are the T count and CT-depth at the $i$-th pair and $\rho=0$ if every rotation angle of the program is a linear function of $\alpha,\beta$ with no constant term ("angle-linear"), else $\rho=1$. **Headline** of an archive: the valid entry with the smallest robust key (ties broken by the entry id; `docs/METHODS.md` §12.4). **GLOBAL BEST**: the entry with the smallest archive key (CX, T, CT-depth, qubits) at the nominal pair only. $\bar T=\frac1{20}\sum_iT_i$ is the mean T count over the 20 pairs. **Clifford offset** (a label of this project, not a standard term): a constant added to a rotation angle that only multiplies the rotation by a Clifford gate; see §3.5. "Headline", "GLOBAL BEST", "robust key" and "T-first" (the entry of smallest $\sum T$, then smallest CX) are also project labels, defined here and not standard terms.

## 1. What was asked
Compress the M9 fuzzy one-Trotter-step circuit (Report 9) with the existing compression machinery, within this session's limits: one ideation pass with at most 4 active families, at most 3 rounds of at most 4 proposers, hard stop for new agent runs at 5 h 15 min (START_HERE §4).

Plan 302 splits the step into two independently compressed units, because the laptop evaluator cannot score a whole fuzzy step (its physical set has about $10^8$ states, Report 9 §2):
- **`fuzzy_ub`** (kind `UB`): $U_B=e^{+2i\beta\square}$ on the 8 row qubits of the 4 inner links, checked as the full $256\times256$ block; up to 32 work qubits allowed.
- **`fuzzy_link`** (kind `UE`): the merged one-link kinetic block $U_K(\kappa_2)U_E(\alpha)=e^{-i(\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathcal K_1+\kappa_2\mathcal K_2)}$ on 4 qubits, checked as the full $16\times16$ block, no work qubits (so it can be reused on every link). In this unit's specification the second angle slot `spec.beta` carries $\kappa_2$, so the three angle pairs are three independent $(\alpha,\kappa_2)$ points and a valid program works for every ratio $r$.

The step is then **composed** (`compose_step`: 12 link blocks + $U_B$, order 1, $r=1$) and re-verified with the M9 step checker.

### 3.5 The headline rule and the "Clifford offsets" (added 2026-11-19; prompts/010, research 007)
**What the offsets are.** In 11 of the 41 valid `fuzzy_ub` entries (class `clifford_offset` in `results/compress/fuzzy_ub/robust.json`; the other 30 are `linear`; all 4 `fuzzy_link` entries are `linear`) some rotation angles are not pure multiples of $\alpha$ or $\beta$ but carry a constant added to them, and every such constant is a multiple of $\pi/2$ (units $-2$, $-3$ or $-6$ in `offset_units`; the GLOBAL BEST `r12-F1-012` has six shifted rotations out of nine, units $-2,-2,-3,-2,-3,-3$; the T-first `r13-F3-013` has three out of seven, units $-3,-2,-2$). Every shifted gate in these 11 entries is a plain uncontrolled `rz` (research 007 §1.2; METHODS §12.4 amendment). For a plain $z$-rotation $R_z(\theta+k\tfrac{\pi}{2})=e^{-ik\pi/4}R_z(\theta)\,S^k$, where $S=\mathrm{diag}(1,i)$ is a Clifford gate (needs no T gate) and the phase $e^{-ik\pi/4}$ is a global phase. So such a constant is a Clifford operation that **costs no T gate**: a gate sequence (the synthesiser's output, called a *word*) with $t$ T gates that approximates $R_z(\theta)$ gives one that approximates $R_z(\theta+k\pi/2)$ with the same $t$. For rotations about $z$ or $x$ or $y$ or a phase gate (`rz`, `rx`, `ry`, `p`) every multiple of $\pi/2$ is Clifford; for controlled rotations (`crz`, `crx`, `cry`, `cp`) only multiples of $\pi$ are, because for example a controlled phase of $\pi/2$ is a controlled-$S$ gate, which costs T. The measured T counts agree: the 20 per-pair T lists of `r12-F1-012` and `r10-F1-010` are identical, $\sum T=12\,688$ for both (research 007 §1.3; this is a measurement with pygridsynth 1.2.0 on these programs, not a proof for every angle). The offsets were chosen in rounds 9–13 to shorten the synthesised circuit at the nominal $\beta=0.61$; together with other changes to the circuit (the archive best is not the headline with offsets added; it descends from a different line, `r11-F1-011`, against `r09-F1-009` for the headline), the archive best reaches CT-depth 1517 against 1546 for the headline. **That nominal depth gain does not survive the 20-pair average**: summed over the 20 pairs the depth of `r12-F1-012` is $30\,908$ against $30\,902$ for `r10-F1-010`, a difference of $+6$ in favour of the headline (`robust.json`; research 007 §1.4). ## 4. What the result means (physics and circuit findings)
- **$U_B$ is cheap once its sector structure is used.** All three U_B families found the same core facts (DISCOVERY lines in `compress/discoveries/fuzzy_ub.md`): (i) after the three-CNOT parity frame, each of the 8 parity sectors becomes $\sigma_p X_f\otimes$(Bell-pair projectors) under an $f$-controlled permutation of the four $q_2$ wires; (ii) these frames need no sector control, because the middle rotation is the identity outside its sector, so neighbouring frames merge (8 controlled swaps in total, DP-optimal over sector order); (iii) the $t=1$ sectors 2, 3, 6, 7 share one frame and one 12-term phase polynomial; (iv) the final step (F3, round 3): a signed relabelling controlled on $p_r\wedge\neg p_t\wedge\neg f$ maps sectors 1, 5 exactly onto 0, 4, removing two controlled swaps, the $p_r$-split one-hot and two middles. A controlled swap (Fredkin) costs 7 CNOTs in Clifford+T, not 8.
- **Negative results** (from exhaustive searches by the proposers): no single routing of $\le4$ controlled $q_2$ swaps brings all 8 sectors into normal form at once (12.7 M routings searched); sectors 0, 1, 4, 5 have no common frame in pairs {0,4}, {1,5}, {0,1}, {4,5}; replacing Toffolis inside Fredkins by relative-phase Toffolis never pays (the leftover phase does not commute with the rotations).
- **The link block is near-optimal at 12 CX** (§3.2).
- After compression the remaining step cost is dominated by the 12 link blocks and by T from rotations. At the 2026-11-06 state: 144 of 295 CX and 148 rotations ($T_{\rm fixed}$ only 118).
