## 1. What was asked
Report 3 built a correct but long circuit for one first-order Trotter step $U_B(\beta)\,U_E(\alpha)$ of the SU(2) plaquette (smallest closed loop of four links) in the spin-network encoding (one qubit per link, one qubit per corner). The task of M4 was to **compress** it with the same method as Report 2: fewer CNOT gates (CX, the two-qubit gate that dominates error-corrected cost), fewer T gates (the expensive non-Clifford gate of the Clifford+T gate set), fewer qubits, while staying exact on all 433 physical (Gauss-law-satisfying) states. The method is the program-search loop of Gu, Kemeny, Yelin and Lukin (arXiv:2610.22367): AI "proposer" agents write small Python programs that build a circuit; a fixed evaluator checks every program exactly and scores it; the best are kept in an archive and fed back to the next round. Required output (prompts/001 §5, prompts/104 §5): a before/after table (general, general + polish, Qiskit level 3, each external seed incl. Paulo's 15-qubit file, best per family, global best, headline), the outer-link electric row, the search history, the verification, and the Lean (proof-assistant) status of every row.

## 2. What was done
- **Machinery.** The evaluator, archive, briefs and report renderer are the ones of the 12-qubit loop (Report 2, `src/su2plaq/compress/`), unchanged. The spin-network encoding was added at run time by a registration module, `src/su2plaq/spinnet/loop.py` ("the shim"; prompts/104 §1), and a spin-network version of the Lean checker (`lean/Su2PlaqCert/SpinNet/`, executable `su2plaq_cert_spinnet`). Evaluator rule (METHODS §9): a program is *valid* only if its error is $\le10^{-10}$ on all 433 states at the nominal angle pair $(\alpha,\beta)=(0.37,0.61)$ and at $(0.91,0.23)$, $(1.70,1.00)$ with the same gate skeleton, ancillas end clean, Gauss's law holds, and the 8 outer-link qubits are only used as controls. Proposer programs run in a sandbox (no file writes, a short import whitelist; erratum E1 made the locked sandbox tests use gate-list programs as real proposers do).
- **Cost key** (METHODS §7, §9), as in Report 2: lexicographic $(\mathrm{CX},\,T_{10^{-6}},\,\mathrm{depth}_{\rm CT},\,\text{qubits})$. $T_{10^{-6}}$ is $T_{\rm fixed}$ (T gates in the circuit as written, e.g. 4 per relative-phase Toffoli `rccx`) plus the T gates that the synthesiser pygridsynth (Ross–Selinger algorithm) needs to build each rotation to accuracy $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$; $\mathrm{depth}_{\rm CT}$ is the depth after synthesis. **CX is compared first, T second.** This ordering is a project choice (prompts/001), not a physical law; see §3.4.
- **Round 0 (seeds)** (`loop seed`, `results/compress/spinnet/round-000.json`): 7 candidates, all valid: the general step of Report 3 (`general`); the same after the loop's polish pass (`general+polish`) and after Qiskit level-3 optimisation in windows of 32 gates (`qiskit_l3`); and four skeletons vendored from the sibling project su2qc-trotter (`spinnet_v2` `pairs9`, `flags4`, `flags5`, `flags6`, placed behind our $U_E$ layer, rotation angles re-solved from $K$ at build time; vendored at sibling commit `feadc42`, Report 3 §3.4). Two external rows are never archived: Paulo's 15-qubit file (fixed angle, $U_B$ only, compared against the target after its recorded sign convention) and the whole-circuit Qiskit level-3 count (not checkable, see §3.1).
- **Ideation** (one proposer session, from the task spec and oracle queries only; `compress/ideation/spinnet.md`): five families. F1: dimer frame with two flags and a 6-term pivot phase polynomial. F2: single flag, corners enter as raw parities. F3: numerical "don't-care" synthesis of the corner-controlled block. F4: in-place re-encoding of dark codes (qubit-count gain only). F5: T-lean phasing on top of F1. Family names and the terms "dimer frame", "pivot", "flag" are the proposers' labels, not standard terms. F4 was never run (its stated gain is only the last key component; session budget). The programs that entered the archive under F3 are, by their file names and discovery lines, flag-and-walk designs; no numerical-synthesis program was found among the 23 candidates (judged from labels, not re-derived).
- **Rounds 1–5** (`prompts/104` §1 protocol): one proposer session per active family. Round 1: F1, F2, F3, F5; round 2: F1, F2, F3, F5; round 3: F1, F2, F3 (F5 rests); round 4: F1, F3 submitted (the F2 session was stopped before it submitted; `notes/STATUS-spinnet-m3-m4.md`); round 5: F1, F2, F3. In total 16 proposer programs (4+4+3+2+3), 23 candidates with the 7 seeds, **all 23 valid** (`summary.json`: `n_valid` 23 of `n_candidates` 23; the round files list `invalid: []`). Proposer sessions: 1 ideation + 4+4+3+3+3 = **18 of the 20 allowed** (tester 118 §2; the round-4 F2 session counts, no archive entry).
- **Headline rule (erratum E3, accepted by Digonto 2026-11-10, `notes/STATUS.md`).** In round 3 two programs lowered the laptop-key $T$ by writing rotations as $R_z(\theta\pm\tfrac\pi4)\,T^{\mp}$ with offsets tuned at the nominal pair; the tester showed the saving (24 T) exists only at that pair ($+13.2\pm2.3$ T on average over 20 random pairs, reports/tests/115). The archive key (the laptop's, measuring $T$ at one pair) was left alone, but **the headline circuit is chosen by a robust key** $(\mathrm{CX},\ \sum_{i=1}^{20}T_{10^{-6}}(\alpha_i,\beta_i),\ \rho,\ \sum_{i=1}^{20}\mathrm{depth}_{\rm CT}(\alpha_i,\beta_i),\ \text{qubits})$, lexicographic. The 20 pairs are `numpy.random.default_rng(20261107)`, 20 values of $\alpha\in[0.05,2.0)$ then 20 of $\beta\in[0.05,1.2)$ (set hash `769caec1c2738356…`, recomputed independently by the auditor and tester). $\rho=0$ if the program is *angle-linear* (every rotation angle is a rational multiple of $\alpha$ or $\beta$, which is what the Lean certificate needs), else $1$. We write $\bar T=\tfrac1{20}\sum T$ ("T_avg" in the tables).
- **Stop.** The METHODS §9 stop rule (3 consecutive rounds without a better *robust* key from round 4 on, per E3; 8 rounds; 20 sessions) fired after round 5: rounds 3, 4, 5 brought no robust improvement (§3.5). This is a rule stop, not a judgement stop (contrast Report 2 §4.3).
- **Lean.** The compiled checker `su2plaq_cert_spinnet` was run on the general step, `pairs9`, and the best entry of rounds 1–2 inside the loop; the headline got a theorem file `lean/certs/spinnet-best-step.lean`. Definitions of the trust labels are in §3.8.

## 3. Results and what they mean
All at $(\alpha,\beta)=(0.37,0.61)$, order 1, $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$ unless stated.

### 3.0 In plain words
The loop found a circuit with **31 CX instead of 54** ($-23$, $-42.6\%$) and **17 qubits instead of 23**, exact on all 433 states, with a Lean certificate of the compiled-checker kind. It did this by **spending T**: its $T_{10^{-6}}$ is $1086$ against $566$ (nominal pair), and averaged over 20 random angle pairs $1092.0$ against $560.6$ ($+94.8\%$). The best of the sibling seeds (`pairs9`, 34 CX) is beaten by 3 CX ($-8.8\%$) at $+28.9\%$ T. So the loop is a CX-for-T trade, not a free lunch; §3.4 shows the whole trade-off curve and says which circuit one would choose under a T-first cost.

### 3.6 What the headline circuit does, and why T went up
Source: the program's own SUMMARY and DISCOVERY lines, `compress/programs/spinnet/F1/002-dimer-linear-corner-walk.py` and `compress/discoveries/spinnet.md` (entry `r02-F1-002`), plus a count of the gate lines of that file (sha256 `750151e9…`). The tester confirmed that the circuit is exact and that the counts are right (reports/tests/114, 118); the reasons below are the proposer's explanation and were **not** independently derived.
1. **Target.** On the 47 admissible inner codes, $K=X^{\otimes4}_{\rm links}\kappa$ on the 8 bright pairs with $\kappa=-2$ (vacuum pair), $-\tfrac12$ (alternating pair), $+1$ (six pairs), and $K=0$ on the 31 dark codes (Report 3 §2, §4; ideation facts 1–2). The outer links are never needed.
2. **Frame.** Three CX ("dimer ladder", the proposer's label) turn the four-link flip $X^{\otimes4}$ into a single $X$ on one qubit (the "pivot", link $b$); the other link qubits then hold $e=y_b\oplus y_r$, $u=y_b\oplus y_t$, $v=y_r\oplus y_l$. In this frame $\kappa=1-A\,(\tfrac94+\tfrac34Z_e)$ with $A=[u=0][v=0]$ (ideation fact 3), so $U_B$ on bright codes is one $X$-rotation of the pivot whose angle depends only on $(A,e)$ and on whether all four corners are zero.
3. **Flags.** One clean ancilla $a_1=[c_0=0][c_2=0]$ (one `rccx`), and $A$ is XORed *in place* into corner qubit $c_0$ by a second `rccx` on a non-clean target (its relative phase cancels with the mirrored uncompute; the corner qubit is restored, so the outer links and all data qubits end unchanged; audit 302 checklist 3). The other corners $c_1$, $c_3$ and $c_0\oplus A$, $a_1$, $e$ are used as plain CX toggles. This saves the third flag and its ancilla of the earlier design (`r01-F1-001`: 32 CX, 18 qubits).
4. **Walk.** After a Hadamard on the pivot, a "Gray-code walk" of 13 CX toggles interleaved with 13 $R_z$ rotations applies a Walsh series (a sum of parity terms) of the angle (Welch et al., New J. Phys. 16, 033040, arXiv:1306.3991, as in Report 3). The walk rotation angles are $\pm\beta,\ \pm\tfrac12\beta,\ \pm\tfrac14\beta,\ \pm\tfrac34\beta$ (the program writes `rz(2cβ)` with $c\in\{\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac14,\pm\tfrac18,\pm\tfrac38\}$), and the four electric rotations are $-\tfrac34\alpha$. $13+4=17$ rotations. A search over the 12-term supports found the all-linear 13-toggle walk (proposer's claim).
5. **Counts, from the program lines.** CX: ladder 3, two `rccx` 6, walk 13, one tail CX 1, two `rccx` uncompute 6, inverse ladder 2: $3+6+13+1+6+2=31$ (count of the program's gate lines; equals the key). $T_{\rm fixed}$: four `rccx` $\times4$ T $=16$ (equals the key). The relative-phase Toffoli (Maslov, arXiv:1508.03273) costs 3 CX and 4 T.

**Why T rose.** $T=T_{\rm fixed}+\sum_rT_r$ with about 63 T per rotation at $10^{-6}$ (§3.1). The general circuit computes seven flags with relative-phase Toffolis (many CX and many fixed T: 56) and uses only 4 walk rotations; the headline removes most flags and instead walks over 13 distinct terms, so $T_{\rm fixed}$ falls by 40 ($56\to16$) but rotation T grows by 560 ($510\to1070$), net $+520$ (trivial differences from §3.2 and §3.1). That is a structural property of the datapath, not an artefact of angle luck: $\bar T=1092.0$ equals the nominal-pair value within 6 T. The proposers' floor claims (`compress/discoveries/spinnet.md`, rounds 3–5, quoted at the scope they state): no 30-CX walk exists in the two-AND F1 design space searched; no 31-CX design with fewer than 13 walk rotations was found in the spaces enumerated; $T$ at fixed CX is set by the number and weights of the walk terms (about 63 T per rotation), so only a 31-CX circuit with 12 or fewer walk rotations could beat the headline on the robust key (r05-F3-005). These are search results over stated design spaces, **not proofs of a 30-CX or 31-CX bound**; the tester spot-checked the quoted counts against the proposers' logs and found all but one (a search count of "118 atom sets" not located in the logs; sanity gap, tester 118 §2).
