## 1. What was asked

Report 5 built a correct but very long circuit for one first-order Trotter step of the SU(2) quantum link model on the plaquette (the smallest closed loop of four links) in the rishon encoding, and its addendum added the determinant ("det") layer. A **Trotter step** is one factor of the product formula that approximates the time evolution $e^{-iHt}$; the compression unit here is the two-layer operator $U=U_B(\beta)\,U_E(\alpha)$ (electric layer first, then the magnetic layer), at the nominal angles $(\alpha,\beta)=(0.37,0.61)$. The task of milestone 6 (M6) was to **compress** that circuit: fewer CX gates (the two-qubit controlled-NOT gate, the main cost on an error-corrected machine), fewer T gates (the expensive non-Clifford gate of the Clifford+T gate set), fewer qubits, while staying exact on all 1296 valid inner link configurations ("inner codes"), which by the inner-block argument of METHODS-rishon R6 covers all 38 432 physical (Gauss-law-satisfying) states.

The method is the program-search loop of Gu, Kemeny, Yelin and Lukin (arXiv:2610.22367), exactly as in Reports 2 and 4: AI "proposer" sessions write small Python programs that build a circuit; a fixed evaluator checks every program exactly and scores it; the best programs are kept in an archive and fed back to the next round. Required output (`prompts/203` §7, `prompts/204` §9): a before/after table; the unit versus the three-layer step; the replay record; both baselines; the prune-cap bookkeeping; the search history; the verification; and the Lean status of every row.

## 2. What was done

- **Machinery.** The evaluator, archive, briefs and report renderer are the laptop modules of the 12-qubit loop (`su2plaq.compress.*`, unchanged); the rishon-specific pieces live in `src/su2plaq/rishon/compress/` (registration, spec text, evaluator, seeds, round runner, replay, three-layer, robust key) and nothing laptop-owned was edited (`prompts/203` §0, METHODS-rishon R10).
- **Validity rule** (METHODS-rishon R10; METHODS §6): a program is *valid* only if (i) it uses allowed gates only, with at most 80 qubits (the sparse simulation engine accepts at most 62; tester 219); (ii) on all 1296 inner codes, at the nominal pair and at the alternates $(0.91,0.23)$ and $(1.70,1.00)$ with the same gate skeleton, the total error `op_norm_error` = block error + leaked amplitude + the engine's certified pruning bound is at most $10^{-10}$; (iii) the pruning bound `dropped_total` is at most the **prune cap** $10^{-11}$ (§3.9); (iv) the Gauss-law residual of the outputs on 273 sampled physical states is at most $10^{-10}$; (v) **no outer qubit is read or written** (the 8 outer links are never needed by inner operators; the evaluator rejects an outer read as `outer_read_unsupported`); (vi) every helper qubit ("ancilla") returns to $|0\rangle$; one global phase. The round's best is also re-verified after Clifford+T synthesis at $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$ with budget $N_{\rm rot}\varepsilon+10^{-9}$.
- **Cost key** (METHODS §7), as in Reports 2 and 4: lexicographic $(\mathrm{CX},\,T_{10^{-6}},\,\mathrm{depth}_{\rm CT},\,\text{qubits})$. $T_{10^{-6}}=T_{\rm fixed}+\sum_rT_r$: $T_{\rm fixed}$ is the T gates of the circuit as written (e.g. 4 per relative-phase Toffoli `rccx`), $T_r$ the T gates that the synthesiser pygridsynth (Ross–Selinger algorithm) needs to build rotation $r$ to accuracy $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$; $\mathrm{depth}_{\rm CT}$ is the depth after synthesis; $N_{\rm rot}$ is the number of rotations that are not multiples of $\pi/4$; $T_{\rm est}=T_{\rm fixed}+3N_{\rm rot}\log_2(1/\varepsilon)$. Qubits = 48 + ancillas (the 32 outer qubits sit in the register and are never touched). **CX is compared first, T second**; this ordering is a project choice (`prompts/001`), not a physical law.
- **Headline rule** (erratum E3, accepted by Digonto 2026-11-10; `prompts/203` §13). The laptop archive key measures $T$ at the single nominal pair, which a rewrite $R_z(\theta)=R_z(\theta\pm\tfrac\pi4)\,T^{\mp}$ can lower by luck. The archive is left as the laptop defines it, but the **headline circuit** is the valid entry with the smallest robust key $(\mathrm{CX},\ \sum_{i=1}^{20}T_{10^{-6}}(\alpha_i,\beta_i),\ \rho,\ \sum_{i=1}^{20}\mathrm{depth}_{\rm CT}(\alpha_i,\beta_i),\ \text{qubits})$ over 20 fixed random pairs (seed 20261107, $\alpha\in[0.05,2.0)$, $\beta\in[0.05,1.2)$, set hash `769caec1…`); $\rho=0$ if every rotation angle is $(m_\alpha\alpha+m_\beta\beta+m_\gamma\sqrt2\beta)/d$ with $d\le64$ ("angle-linear", what the Lean certificate needs), else $1$. $\bar T=\tfrac1{20}\sum T$ is called "T_avg". In this loop the archive best and the headline are the same entry, so the choice never mattered (§3.1).

### 3.0 In plain words
The loop turned a circuit of **10 102 CX** into one with **216 CX**: $46.8\times$ fewer, or $-97.9\%$ (trivial ratio of the two cited numbers); against the cheapest valid starting circuit of round 0 (the rccx ladder, 6 874 CX) it is $31.8\times$ fewer ($-96.9\%$). The qubit count falls from 58 to 52 (the circuit touches 20 qubits: 16 inner plus 4 helpers; the other 32 outer qubits are untouched). (Qubit conventions: the compression keys count the full register, 48/52/58 qubits; the general-circuit files `step.json` and `UD.json` count only the logical qubits the circuit acts on, 16/26. Both are quoted with their source.) $T_{10^{-6}}$ falls from 20 650 to **832** ($24.8\times$ fewer), and averaged over 20 random angle pairs from 20 620.2 to 829.8. Unlike the 12-qubit and spin-network compressions (Reports 2 and 4), this is **not** a trade of CX for T: the headline has the smallest CX count and the smallest nominal $T$ of all 23 valid circuits (§3.4). The circuit is exact on all 1296 inner codes at three angle pairs (error $1.35\times10^{-13}$, a factor 740 below the tolerance), and carries a Lean certificate for the angle-linear gate family it builds, valid for all real $(\alpha,\beta)$ (that the program emits this family at every angle pair is confirmed at 25 pairs, not proved; §3.6). With the det layer added in front (the full one-Trotter-step of the model with the det term) it is **248 CX, $T=1328$, 52 qubits**.

### 3.2 Unit versus three-layer step (`prompts/204` §6.1, §9)
The model of Report 5's addendum has three Trotter layers, $U_B(\beta)\,U_D(\eta)\,U_E(\alpha)$, where $U_D(\eta)=e^{-i\eta H_D^{\rm in}}$ is the determinant ("det") layer, $\eta=\lambda_D\,dt$ is its angle and $\lambda_D$ is the coefficient of the det term in the Hamiltonian ($\gamma$ is **not** used for it in this project; it keeps naming the $\sqrt2\beta$ slot of the angle rule). Because $[U_D,U_E]=0$ exactly, the three-layer step equals $[U_BU_E]\,U_D$: **the det layer is put in front of the compressed unit** (`prepend_det_layer`). The det layer was not sent through the loop: it is four copies of a 4-qubit gadget already at the rotation minimum, and merging it with $U_E$ could save at most 2 CX per link, 8 CX of 248 (`prompts/204` §6.1). Nominal angle triple $(\alpha,\beta,\eta)=(0.37,0.61,0.29)$; alternates $(0.91,0.23,0.53)$ and $(1.70,1.00,1.07)$. $\bar T$ for the det layer is averaged over 20 values of $\eta$ (seed 20261110, $\eta\in[0.05,1.2)$). **What each round found, in plain words.** Every bright pair (a pair of inner codes joined by the magnetic term $H_B^{\rm in}$; there are 81, with coupling strength $\kappa\in\{1,2,4\sqrt2\}$, while 990 codes are "dark", i.e. untouched by it) must be rotated by an angle $\kappa\beta$; the general circuit does this with one multi-controlled rotation per pair (166 rotations, 10 102 CX). The ideas below are the proposers' own explanations from the program SUMMARY and DISCOVERY lines (`compress/archive/rishon/entries/*.json`, `compress/discoveries/rishon.md`); the tester confirmed that every circuit is exact and every count is right, **not** the explanations.
1. **Round 1, `r01-F1-001`: one "flip qubit" for all 81 pairs.** A fixed reversible "frame" (CX and Toffoli gates) rewrites the codes so that, for every bright pair, the two members differ only on one qubit $F$ (the proposer's label: the Q-flag of the bottom link). Then $e^{i\beta\kappa X_F}$ for all pairs becomes one rotation on $F$ whose angle is chosen by a few flag qubits (a "uniformly controlled rotation", 8 terms, 8 CX), and the frame is undone. 256 CX instead of 10 102.
2. **Round 2, `r02-F3-001`: the controlled partner map instead of a frame.** An involution ("partner map" $\Pi$: applying it twice does nothing) sends each member of a bright pair to the other. With one extra flip qubit as the test qubit, $e^{i\theta\Pi}$ can be built as a controlled $\Pi$, a rotation $e^{i\theta X_F}$, and the controlled $\Pi$ again (a Hadamard-test identity). It needs no canonical frame and uses fewer qubits than the F1 frame (54 against 57–58).
3. **Round 3: cheaper partner map and exact corner tests.** F3 (`r03-F3-002`): the controlled-$\Pi$ becomes $W\,Z_E\,W$ with $W=\prod_c CH(\lnot e_{2,c}\to e_{1,c})$ (a product of four controlled Hadamards), which needs no gate controlled on $F$ (16 instead of 24 CX), and one fold of a dark label onto $F$ replaces two folds. F1 (`r03-F1-003`): the "bright test" (the AND of four corner conditions that tells bright codes from dark ones) is made exact with in-place tests, dropping the rotation count from 14 to 10 (T 1150 to 870).
4. **Round 4, "class selector": 6 rotation terms instead of 10.** The rotation angle of every bright pair depends only on two flags: $P=[r_t=0\ \text{and}\ r_r=r_l]$ (true for the $\kappa=1$ class and the closed pair) and $Z=[r=000]$ (the closed pair). The signed angle takes three values, $-2\beta$, $-\beta$, $4\sqrt2\beta$ (for $P=0$; $P=1,Z=0$; $Z=1$), so the Walsh series (a sum of parity-product terms; one rotation per term) has 3 terms instead of 5 and the rotation on $F$ is a 6-term Gray-code walk instead of a 10-term one. F1 and F3 found it independently (`r04-F1-004`, `r04-F3-003`; the same circuit, 228 CX, $N_{\rm rot}=10$).
5. **Round 5, "closed-sector skip" and "CY gauge merge" with RC3X.** F3 (`r05-F3-005`): the exact 6-CX controlled Hadamard that undoes a corner operation on the closed loop is replaced by *skipping* that corner operation on the closed sector with a 2-CX toggle and restoring a lost phase with T before and T$^\dagger$ after (220 CX). F1 (`r05-F1-005`): a controlled-Z phase gauge is merged into an existing CX so that $CZ\cdot CX=S\,CY$ costs one CX instead of two; and a Maslov relative-phase 3-control X (RC3X, 6 CX, 8 T) replaces two `rccx`, at equal cost and with one ancilla fewer (226 CX, 52 qubits). F4 (`r05-F4-005`): a set-cover search shrinks its bright test from 37 to 29 CX per side (274 CX).
6. **Round 6: combine them.** F1 (`r06-F1-006`) applies both F3's skip and its own CY merge (218 CX). F3 (`r06-F3-006`) merges the "closed-dark mark" into the restore: the restore CX becomes an `rccx` whose extra control $\lnot q_3$ does the job of a separate fold (+2 CX, $-3$ CX), and carries over F1's CY merge and the RC3X test: **216 CX, $T=832$, 52 qubits**.

### 3.8 Lean status of the circuits
**Definitions.** *Kernel-checked*: Lean's small trusted core re-verified the proof by computation, with only the standard axioms. *Native-certified* (the label used here for per-circuit certificates): a compiled Lean program (the checker `su2plaq_rishon_cert`, written in Lean) accepted an exported certificate, and the theorem file proves `checkJsonString certJson = true` by `native_decide`, so the trust is in the Lean compiler and the checker source, not only in the kernel. **The headline is native-certified, not kernel-checked.** `certified` in the tables = native-certified; `not run` = the loop made no certificate; `n/a` = no certificate is possible or meaningful.
- **What is checked** (METHODS-rishon R12; tester 434 §2). A certificate is a gate list, a target (a $1296\times1296$ matrix written with exact numbers) and a global phase. The checker runs the gates exactly, symbolically in three angle variables $z_\alpha=e^{i\alpha/d_\alpha}$, $z_\beta=e^{i\beta/d_\beta}$, $z_\gamma=e^{i\sqrt2\beta/d_\gamma}$ ($\gamma$ here is the $\sqrt2\beta$ slot) over $K=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$, on each of the 1296 inner codes, and demands that the result equal the phase times the target column exactly, with ancillas back at 0 and nothing outside the code space. An identity of Laurent polynomials holds under every substitution of the variables, so in particular for **all real** $(\alpha,\beta)$, not only at the nominal pair (the irrationality of $\sqrt2$ is needed only for the converse: that a numerical match at all angles would force the polynomial identity). Scope: the certificate covers the angle-linear gate family with the nominal gate skeleton; that the *program* emits this family is confirmed at the 20 + 3 pairs of the evaluator and 2 more in audit 307, not proved for every $(\alpha,\beta)$ (audit 307 N2).
- **Headline `r06-F3-006`: `certified`.** Certificate `compress/lean/certs/step-o1-24d16bdd9c45.json` (sha256 `63a96a40…` after the regeneration at `072cdfe`, `ef70932c…` before; the gate list is the same (its sha256 `24d16bdd…` is the circuit id), the provenance block names the new commit and exporter hash; 662 gates, 20 qubits, base $(d_\alpha,d_\beta,d_\gamma)=(4,4,1)$; made in 12.8 s; the file is gitignored, the theorem file embeds it). - **The three-layer row is a composition of two Lean-certified parts, not one Lean theorem** (METHODS-rishon R16). If circuit 1 maps each inner code $c$ to $k_1\sum_{c'}(V_1)_{c'c}|c'\rangle$ with ancillas 0, and circuit 2 does the same with $V_2$, then circuit 1 followed by circuit 2 maps $c$ to $k_1k_2\sum_{c'}(V_2V_1)_{c'c}|c'\rangle$ by linearity (the intermediate state is a superposition of inner codes with clean ancillas). So the unit certificate (for $V_1=U_BU_E$ at all $(\alpha,\beta)$) and the det-layer certificate (for $V_2=U_D$ at all $\eta$) together give the three-layer step for all $(\alpha,\beta,\eta)$ with no fourth variable. - **Where the angle-fit rule applies** (tester 434 N1). ### 3.10 Context (not like for like)
Reports 2 and 4 compress other encodings: the 12-qubit encoding (Report 2: CX $294\to111$, $T=912$, 31 qubits) and the spin-network encoding (Report 4: CX $54\to31$, $T=1086$, 17 qubits). ## 4. Findings
The `DISCOVERY:` lines that survived (full text in `compress/discoveries/rishon.md`; all are the proposers' statements, checked only as far as the circuits they built are exact):
- Every bright pair can be mapped to a pair that differs in **one** qubit with a fixed CX/Toffoli frame, so all 81 rotations become one multiplexed rotation (F1, round 1); the closed ($\kappa=4\sqrt2$) pair needs its own 16-CX gadget there.
- The partner map is an involution on the 81 pairs; the controlled partner involution needs no F-controlled gate ($Q=WZ_EW$, 16 instead of 24 CX; F3, round 3); one dark-label fold onto $F$ replaces two (F3, round 3).
- The angle of every bright pair depends only on two flags $(P,Z)$, so 6 Walsh rotation terms suffice (round 4). For the flags $(x,k,z,y)$ of round 2 an exhaustive search over supports of up to 9 terms found none, so 10 terms was the minimum there; the class selector reaches 6 by changing the flags.
- The closed-loop corner Hadamard can be skipped by a 2-CX toggle with a T/T$^\dagger$ phase restore (F3, round 5); a CZ gauge merges into a CX as $S\,CY$ (F1, round 5); a Maslov RC3X replaces two `rccx` (F1, round 5); the closed-dark mark merges into the restore (F3, round 6).
- **Floors, as the proposers state them, are search results and not proofs.** F4's own analysis puts its floor at $\ge244$ CX for its design, against the F3 line's 232 at the time (round 4); the tester checked only the combinatorial sub-claim (reports/tests/221). For the headline, "no single symmetric gate deletion of the 216-CX circuit stays exact" is true for every gate that costs something (§3.7). No proof of a 216-CX lower bound exists or is claimed.
- The $U_E$ phases cannot leave the Clifford+T critical path in this skeleton: $U_E$ does not commute with $U_B$ (33 of the 81 bright pairs mix codes with different numbers of Q links), and the best placement found gives depth$_{\rm CT}$ 1358 for F1 (the Walsh chain alone is 1228).
